## What was built
Four ways of stepping a spring forward in time (Euler, RK4, symplectic Euler, velocity Verlet)
compared on what they do to the energy over 200 swings, and a projectile study that now also
measures how well each method places the landing between two steps. In the proper terms: a
model-free fixed-step driver in `numerics/`, used by two models (the oscillator and the
projectile); `POST /api/v1/physics/oscillator/integrators` and the page
`/physics/oscillator/integrators`. The three projectile pages gained per-run checks (v1.1.0).
Plan: `docs/plans/ROADMAP_2_4_PLAN.md`; guide: Part 21.

## What it shows, and how we know
| Claim | How it was checked | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Euler multiplies the energy by exactly 1 + theta^2 per step; RK4 by 1 - theta^6/72 + theta^8/576 | exact rational arithmetic (`fractions`, 40 steps); 50-digit `decimal` for E_N/E_0 | Proved, Tested |
| Symplectic Euler and Verlet each conserve a shadow energy H~ exactly | exact `fractions` iteration at theta = 1/5 and 3/2; in floats within a measured rounding allowance (10^5 steps) | Proved, Tested |
| Their true energy stays within theta/(1 - theta/2) or (theta^2/4)/(1 - theta^2/4) of E_0 for theta < 2 | derived by hand in the plan; 40 random float cases | Proved (exact arithmetic), Tested (floats) |
| Measured orders: Euler 1.013, RK4 4.000, symplectic Euler 1.014, Verlet 2.000 | fits of the maximum error over all steps, default request | Observed |
| Measuring only at t = T gives order 2 for symplectic Euler, so the study fits over all steps | closed-form discrete map: endpoint slope 2 +/- 0.05, max-over-steps slope 1 +/- 0.05 | Tested |
| Landing time: RK4 order 2.000 (linear) and 4.039 (Hermite); Verlet 2.000 and 2.000; Euler 0.997 and 0.998 | fits against `analytical.flight_time`, default request | Observed |
| Linear and Hermite location errors match s(1 - s)h^2 g''/(2\|g'\|) and s^2(1 - s)^2 h^4 g''''/(24\|g'\|) | e^t - 2 at the mid-step, within 1% at the finest h; the projectile's signs and sizes within 1e-3 | Tested (standard result, not proved here) |
| Moving the projectile study and both landing solvers onto shared code changed nothing | golden digests unchanged before the version bump; old and new `integrate()` equal on 1530 cases | Tested |
| The checks' thresholds (500, 20, 10, 50) are measured, not derived | worst cases over grids (for example 1.014 over 24 182 runs for the modified-energy one); the 50 has a stated caveat near theta = 2 (SCIENTIFIC_VALIDATION.md) | Observed |
| A planted fault is caught (Verlet's second half-kick at the old position; a wrong Hermite slope; a non-Runge-Kutta stepper) | mutant tests | Tested |

## One figure to reproduce
Page `/physics/oscillator/integrators`, the default request: m = 1 kg, k = 25 N/m (omega = 5
rad/s), x0 = 0.1 m, v0 = 0, eight time steps from 0.04 s halved seven times, all four methods,
energy step 0.05 s (theta = 0.25), 200 periods. Look for: parallel lines of slope 1, 2 and 4 on
the convergence chart, and on the energy chart Euler's envelope climbing while symplectic Euler
and Verlet stay in bounded bands.

## Two-minute demo
1. Open `/physics/oscillator/integrators` from the nav bar and press Run.
2. Convergence chart: say "the lines are straight with slopes 1, 2 and 4, and the fitted orders
   are 1.013, 2.000, 1.014 and 4.000". The check behind it: `order_<method>` (within 0.1 of
   theory; measured, not a proof).
3. Energy chart: say "Euler gains energy every step, by exactly 1 + theta^2; the symplectic
   methods wobble but never drift". The checks: `energy_identity_euler` (exact identity),
   `modified_energy_<method>` and `energy_bound_<method>` (proved bound plus rounding).
4. Say why the order is fitted over all steps: the endpoint alone would call symplectic Euler
   second order (a tested trap). Open a projectile page and show the landing-order checks.

## Three questions you should be able to answer
1. Why does a method that is only first order still keep the energy bounded for ever?
2. What would go wrong if the landing were placed on a step node instead of mid-step?
3. Which of this step's numbers are proved, which tested and which only observed?
