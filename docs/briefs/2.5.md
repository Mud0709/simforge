## What was built
A driven spring is "measured" the way a lab would: for each drive frequency the simulator starts
the spring from rest, waits for the start-up wobble (the transient) to die away, then reads the
steady amplitude and phase lag. The dots sit over the exact resonance formula and the peak is
refined. In the proper terms: a model-free sweep driver (`numerics/sweep.py`) now serves the two
angle sweeps and the new `physics.oscillator.resonance_sweep`; `POST
/api/v1/physics/oscillator/resonance-sweep` and the "Simulated resonance curve" panel on
`/physics/oscillator`. Plan: `docs/plans/ROADMAP_2_5_PLAN.md`; guide: Part 22.

## What it shows, and how we know
| Claim | How it was checked | Label |
|---|---|---|
| The lock-in returns A cos(delta) and A sin(delta) exactly for a sinusoid over whole periods (R3) | derived on paper; synthetic sinusoids, M = 3, 4, 64 samples per period, eight phases | Proved, Tested |
| In steady state sin(delta) = b w A / F0: drive power equals damping power (R4) | derived on paper (energy per period); checked at every point of the test grids | Proved, Tested |
| A signal error e moves the amplitude by at most 2e; the transient left after the wait moves it by at most eps_s A (R5) | derived on paper; tests on a known decaying term | Proved, Tested |
| The simulated amplitude and phase lie within bounds B1 and arcsin(B1/A) of the formula (C1, C2, C3), and the power balance holds within its bound (C4) | proved given the measured trajectory error e_x against the exact solution (so not a bound on the solver); worst point used 0.198 of C1's bound and 0.218 of C2's at the default | Proved (given e_x), Observed (sizes) |
| The rounding allowance r_A is large enough | its form differs from the plan's (it grows with the run length); the float lock-in of the exact motion differed from a long-double one by at most 0.0063 r_A over 432 points | Observed (a margin, not a proof) |
| The refined peak lies within max(u, xatol) of the exact w_r (C5) | u is an estimate from the grid's curvature, not a bound; value/limit 9.0e-4 on the default grid, none on the zeta = 0.9 and 0.5 grids | Observed |
| Wrapped phase differences are used, so a lag near pi does not read as 2 pi | a planted s < 0 near pi passes wrapped and fails raw | Tested |
| The two angle sweeps behave the same after moving onto the shared driver | golden digests and all existing sweep tests unchanged | Tested |
| A sweep cannot run for minutes: Q <= 300, 201 frequencies, 1,000,000 evaluations, DOP853 or RK45 only | slowest refusal 12.3 s, slowest admitted 8.7 s, on one machine (half the 30 s proxy limit kept as margin) | Observed (one machine) |
| The page shows the dashed exact curve, the dots, the refined peak with its estimate, and absent (not 0) values when no exact peak exists | component tests with a mocked server (52 passed across the three panel test files); the Playwright specs are run by the full check, not recorded here | Tested (components) |

Not shown: the cross-machine replay rules (derived, not run on a second machine) and the effect of a
coarse grid (grid step above gamma) on C5. One plan item was not done: the angle panel was not
generalised to any swept variable; the resonance panel is a separate component.

## One figure to reproduce
Page `/physics/oscillator`, panel "Simulated resonance curve", all defaults: m = 1 kg, k = 25 N/m
(omega0 = 5 rad/s), b = 1 kg/s (zeta = 0.1, Q = 5), F0 = 1 N, drive 0.5 to 15 rad/s, 21
frequencies, settle tolerance 1e-6, DOP853. Look for: the dots on the dashed curve, a peak near the
exact omega_r = 4.9497 rad/s with A_max = 0.2010 m (formula values), and the checks table.

## Two-minute demo
1. Open `/physics/oscillator`, scroll to "Simulated resonance curve" and press "Sweep drive
   frequency".
2. Point at the chart: say "each dot is a separate run from rest, read after waiting out the
   transient; the dashed line is the formula". The check behind it: `lock_in_amplitude` (proved
   given the measured trajectory error).
3. Point at the peak row: say "the refined peak comes with an accuracy estimate, labelled as an
   estimate". The check: `peak_frequency` (observed, not a proof).
4. Say how the phase is checked without the amplitude formula: the power balance `power_balance`
   (energy per period in equals energy lost to damping).
5. Set the damping so that zeta = 0.8 and sweep again: the exact peak shows as absent, not 0.

## Three questions you should be able to answer
1. Why does the sweep wait a whole number of drive periods before reading, and what does waiting
   longer cost?
2. Why is the lock-in exact on a sinusoid, and what does "proved given the measured error" leave
   unproved?
3. Which of this step's numbers are proved, which tested and which only observed?
